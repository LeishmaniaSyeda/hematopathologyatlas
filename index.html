Two more visual systems

Marrow and lymph node

Bone marrow: what to orient to

  • Cellularity: proportion of hematopoietic cells to fat, interpreted for age and specimen quality.
  • Myeloid-to-erythroid ratio: a contextual estimate, not a diagnosis by itself.
  • Megakaryocytes: number, distribution, size, nuclear lobulation and clustering.
  • Reticulin / collagen: fibrosis grading requires an appropriate trephine and stain.
  • Aspirate vs trephine: cytology and differential count versus architecture, cellularity and fibrosis.
A haemodilute aspirate can under-represent marrow disease. Correlate aspirate morphology with the core biopsy and clinical picture.

Lymph node: low power before high power

  • Capsule and sinuses: preserved or effaced architecture?
  • Follicles: primary / secondary, polarized or monotonous, variable or back-to-back.
  • Paracortex: T-cell-rich zone; may expand in viral/reactive conditions.
  • Medulla: cords and sinuses, plasma cells and macrophages.
  • Background: eosinophils, histiocytes, plasma cells, granulomas or fibrosis.
Architecture is diagnostic evidence. A cytology-only view can miss the pattern that separates a reactive node from a lymphoma.

Disease atlas · erythrocytes and marrow

Anemia atlas

Study anemias by mechanism, then use MCV, reticulocyte response and morphology to organise the differential.

A low Hb is a finding, not an aetiology. Interpret RBC indices, reticulocytes, smear, iron/B12/folate studies and clinical context together. MCV categories overlap and mixed deficiencies can obscure the pattern.
Disease atlas · myeloid and lymphoid neoplasia

Leukemia & myeloid atlas

Recognise blast morphology, lineage, maturation patterns and the marrow / blood picture. The myeloid neoplasm section also includes MPN and MDS/MPN entities.

Morphology establishes a working pattern; classification often requires flow cytometry, cytogenetics and molecular testing. WHO-HAEM5 and ICC 2022 are not identical in every entity or threshold.
Disease atlas · lymphoid tissue

Lymphoma & lymphoid atlas

Start at low power. Identify the architecture, then zoom into the diagnostic cells and interpret the immunophenotype.

Terminology evolves. For example, WHO-HAEM5 uses “nodular lymphocyte-predominant B-cell lymphoma” (NLPBCL), while ICC retains “nodular lymphocyte-predominant Hodgkin lymphoma” (NLPHL). This atlas flags important naming differences instead of pretending classifications are identical.
Diagnostic laboratory

IHC & cytochemistry

Markers answer questions about lineage, maturation, differentiation and proliferation. Interpret panels, not isolated positives.

A marker is not a diagnosis. Expression can be aberrant, heterogeneous, technically variable or shared across entities. Use morphology, a coherent panel and the correct tissue context.
